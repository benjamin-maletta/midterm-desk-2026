The national picture

What the environment looks like four days out, and why so many Republican seats are in play.

Environment

  • Generic ballot averages around D +9 (DDHQ aggregate, through Oct 26). Midterm environments of that size typically cost the president's party dozens of House seats.
  • Trump's disapproval sat at 61.2% in Cook's tracker as of Oct 15, with the Iran war and cost of living as the dominant drags.
  • Cook moved 14 House and Senate races toward Democrats on Oct 30 and now sketches a Democratic House gain of roughly 12 to 25 seats.
  • Senate control is a narrower question: Democrats need a net +4 (a 50-50 tie goes to VP Vance).

What that means for the Senate

Democrats are defending 13 seats, two of them (Michigan, Georgia) in states Trump carried. Republicans are defending 22, and six of those are genuine toss-ups. The likeliest outcomes cluster around a 50-50 or 51-49 Senate, which is why the toss-up column is worth more than the national number.

Where the ratings disagree

Cook is the reference rating on every card, but the raters are not unanimous. The spread matters most in Alaska, Ohio, Maine, Texas, Kansas, and North Carolina, where at least one forecaster has moved a state a full category past Cook.

Notes on the House

This desk does not model individual House districts. The friend's original file kept a national group-vote baseline on a House tab; that baseline has been replaced here with the generic ballot and the Cook seat range, which are the two numbers that actually move district-level expectations.

How to read this (and what's missing)

      
Inherited almost verbatim from the original file, because the caveats are the honest part.

      
Each race card pulls what was publicly visible from Cook Political Report, RealClearPolling, CNN, NYT/Siena, Silver Bulletin and Pod Save America as of Oct 30. "No item found" means no race-specific public item was found, not that the source has nothing. Paywalled material (Cook's race pages, Silver's per-race odds table, NYT articles, subscriber-only podcast episodes) is excluded unless reported publicly elsewhere. "Reference margin" is one polling average picked for comparison and labeled on each card; it is not a forecast. Pod Save America is an openly Democratic-aligned advocacy show, not a forecaster.

      
Poll tables

      
Poll rows come from the Wikipedia race pages, which compile the public October polls. A poll is included if its field period ended in October 2026 (a few September polls are kept where October was thin). RCP does not grade pollsters, and neither does this page. The "Higher-quality" tag is a screen: independent media, university or nonpartisan pollsters that publish their methods and are not sponsored by a campaign or party committee. "Other" covers campaign- or party-sponsored polls and pollsters with a consistent partisan house effect, plus any that could not be classified. It is a screen, not a verdict, and a few "Other" pollsters have decent records.

      
Reference margin

      
The number in the box is a plain average of the listed polls' two-way margins (Democratic minus Republican), with third-party and undecided shares set aside. No house-effect adjustments, no recency weighting, no likely-voter correction. A positive number means the Democrat led the average.

      
State profiles and history

      
PVI, last statewide result, and incumbent status are from Cook/Wikipedia. Historic margins are rounded. Group-level vote patterns are typical patterns, not state exit-poll readings, so they are not repeated here.
